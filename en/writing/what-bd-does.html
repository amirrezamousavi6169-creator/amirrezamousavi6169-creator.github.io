What a business developer actually does

Most people picture the wrong job. Here is the difference between closing a deal and building the thing that makes deals possible.

Most people think a business developer is someone who goes out, does marketing, performs a small miracle, and comes back having signed the biggest, most profitable contract imaginable. That picture is wrong, and it is exactly why so many organisations never use the role properly.

A business developer is the architect of an organisation, not the labourer of a contract. We create the conditions for that big, profitable deal to exist. We do not sit down to sign it, and we do not chase someone with clever tricks to get their signature on a piece of paper. We build the structure inside which the deal becomes possible.

An example from my own work

A few years ago I worked with a tourism organisation that had a problem at its root: it waited for government funding and did not even have money to buy chairs. The common assumption was that the solution was to find one big sponsor. But the sponsor was never the problem. The problem was that the organisation had no structure for generating income at all.

What I did was not to close a deal. I designed four revenue paths the organisation had never even considered:

  • Putting usable historic buildings to work as cafes, boutique stays and training spaces, restored through heritage funding and operated for revenue.
  • A premium tier of city tours with special routes and services for a specific kind of visitor.
  • Sponsorship and advertising, which was far easier to set up than anyone assumed.
  • Support for local makers and independent sellers, framed as a recurring festival rather than a one-off.

None of these was a contract. Each one was a structure, something that could produce contracts again and again.

The difference that matters

This is the core distinction. A contract labourer brings in money once. An organisation architect builds a system that generates money on its own, even after they have gone.

That is also why business development cannot be measured only by the deals signed this quarter. The real output is capacity: the routes, the partnerships, and the internal structure that make next year deals inevitable rather than lucky.

So when someone asks what a business developer does, the honest answer is not that they sell. It is closer to this: they look at a business, find where it is leaking value, and build the structure that closes the gap.
